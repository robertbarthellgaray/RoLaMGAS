ⓘ C3 and launch-vehicle capability

C3 = |vspacecraft − vEarth|² at departure, in km²/s². It is the square of Earth-relative hyperbolic excess speed, not the parking-orbit burn. Only the first-leg energy is checked for an out-of-range trial; the remaining trajectory is not evaluated. New searches start on the selected C3 boundary and refine within the bounds. Use a minimum C3 and an arrival date to explore a high-energy historical mission.

The application floor is 6.25 km²/s², derived from the JUICE daytime-launch design reference V∞ = 2.5 km/s. BepiColombo’s specified launch V∞ = 3.475 km/s gives C3 ≈12.08. These are design references, not a universal physical lower bound. JUICE source · BepiColombo source

A plot shows position, not speed. Similar-looking paths can have different dates, revolution counts and departure velocities. The inspector lists the Earth-relative vector and each Lambert branch.

Reference: launch-mass chart supplied from the Uranus mission report. These are study assumptions, not current certified launch-vehicle performance or a payload filter. The plotted range ends at C3 = 100.

ⓘ DSM, mismatch, arrival V∞ and flight time

DSM is an intentional deep-space maneuver. The total budget includes designed DSMs and any modeled powered flybys. Unpowered flybys must join with equal incoming/outgoing V∞ magnitudes and a feasible turn above the altitude floor.

A large mismatch means the legs do not connect as an unpowered trajectory. A card is marked “Unresolved” only when an unmodeled flyby speed mismatch exceeds 100 m/s. Smaller mismatches retain the numeric modeled burn total; the detailed mismatch checks still apply. Enable powered flybys to construct and budget explicit periapsis burns. The combined DSM screening budget also includes the sum of unresolved flyby speed mismatches. This is a screening metric, not the magnitude of a constructed periapsis burn. Zero designed DSM does not mean zero repair cost: the mismatched flyby and finite-patch export may require additional burns. Red candidates remain diagnostics; inspect the integrated burn table before treating a candidate as flyable.

Arrival V∞ is speed relative to the destination before capture. Flyby probes can tolerate higher speeds; orbiters typically need lower speeds to reduce insertion cost. The presets are editable search suggestions, not automatic capture designs.

Target arrival date

Blank uses leg-duration ranges. A date with tolerance 0 fixes the encounter at 00:00 TDB that day and replaces the final-leg duration bounds. For an exact time, use the encounter windows below. Flight-time and flyby constraints still apply.

Historical encounter date windows

One line per encounter in route order, including launch and arrival. Enter YYYY-MM-DD or ISO date/time; use two comma-separated values for a window, or one for an exact epoch. This constrains flyby dates as well as arrival. Clear this box to return to duration-based searches.

Individual burn limits

Explicit common-periapsis impulse above each altitude floor. A conservative feasible construction, not a minimum-fuel burn.

Minimum flyby altitudes

Earth and Venus: low-altitude passes may encounter atmosphere, drag and heating. These limits are geometric floors; atmospheric effects are not modeled. Jupiter: radiation exposure depends on the encounter path and time spent near the planet. This solver does not calculate radiation dose.

Local computation

Local refinement improves candidates; it does not prove a global optimum. Arrival V∞ is constrained at the selected destination.

A retrograde target does not require retrograde cruise: a fast flyby can use either. “Both” searches both senses with the selected branch budget.
